# Correction Prompt

You previously produced a neutral first-century reading of **[CHAPTER]**. That reading has been reviewed, and one or more passages require re-analysis because the original reading either misidentified a textual element or failed to follow the evidence the text provides.

Below is the specific issue. Re-analyze the passage in question using the same methodology as the original reading: first-century tools only (LXX, Targums, first-century writings), no post-first-century theology, close attention to the Greek, and the author's own usage as the primary guide to meaning.

## What to re-examine

**Passage:** [VERSE RANGE]

**Problem:** [FLAG THE PROBLEM, NOT THE SOLUTION — e.g., "The analysis identifies chrysoun thymiaterion as a 'golden altar of incense' and then treats the censer reading as a secondary alternative. Please reconsider which items belonged in which place. Follow the author's own argument: he is cataloguing what belongs to the inner sanctuary."]

## Rules

1. You are correcting your own prior analysis. Do not defend the original reading — re-examine the evidence and follow it.
2. The correction prompt identifies the *problem*. You determine the *answer* from the text. If you re-examine the evidence and still arrive at the original reading, say so and explain why.
3. Produce a replacement for the relevant section of the original analysis — same depth, same format, same methodology. The replacement should drop seamlessly into the existing chapter file.
4. Do not alter any section of the analysis that is not affected by this correction.
