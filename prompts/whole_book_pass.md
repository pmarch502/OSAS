# Whole-Book Pass Prompt

You are producing a structural analysis of a single book of the Bible. This analysis will serve as the foundation for detailed chapter-by-chapter exegesis that follows. Your task is to read the entire book as a single unit and produce a document that maps the author's argument, identifies key terms, and traces the coherence of the book from beginning to end.

## Methodology

- First-century tools only: LXX, Second Temple literature, Targums, first-century writings.
- No post-first-century theology or doctrine. Do not import any theological framework developed after the first century.
- The author's own usage is the primary guide to meaning. When a term appears multiple times in the book, its meaning is determined by how the author uses it across the entire work — not by how later interpreters defined it.
- Close attention to the Greek (or Hebrew/Aramaic for OT books).

## What to produce

### 1. Occasion and Audience
- Who is the author writing to?
- What situation or crisis prompted this writing?
- What is the author trying to accomplish?

### 2. Argument Flow
- Map the book's argument or narrative from beginning to end in a structured outline.
- For each major section, state what the author is arguing and how it advances the overall purpose.
- Identify the transitions — how does each section connect to the next?

### 3. Key Terms
- Identify every significant term or phrase that the author uses repeatedly or that carries weight in the argument.
- For each term, trace its usage across the entire book. Cite every occurrence.
- State what the author's own usage reveals about the term's meaning. Where the author's usage constrains or specifies the meaning, say so explicitly.
- If a term has a range of meaning in Greek/Hebrew but the author's usage in this book narrows it, identify the narrowed meaning and the evidence.

### 4. Internal Coherence
- Identify any places where the author's argument in one section depends on, clarifies, or constrains the interpretation of another section.
- Note where a term introduced early in the book is defined or clarified by the author's usage later in the book (or vice versa).
- The goal is to ensure that the chapter-by-chapter exegesis that follows will interpret each part consistently with the whole.

### 5. Injections Needed
- Are there any places where the author's argument depends on background knowledge that a modern reader (or AI) might not have or might get wrong due to post-first-century theological assumptions?
- Flag these explicitly. These are places where the chapter-by-chapter exegesis will need specific guidance to avoid importing later theology.

## Rules

- Work from the biblical text itself. Do not summarize what scholars say about the book — analyze the text.
- Do not flatten the author's argument into a single theme. If the book addresses multiple issues, map all of them.
- Do not assume you know what a term means from other biblical books or from theological tradition. Let this author's usage in this book be the primary guide.
- Be specific. Cite verses. Quote the Greek/Hebrew where it matters.
- This document will be used by agents who have not read the book. It must be detailed enough that an agent reading only this document would understand the book's argument, key terms, and internal logic.
